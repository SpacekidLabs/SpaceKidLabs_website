# Writing archive posts

The archive reads like a lab notebook: someone explaining a technique they work with, plainly, to another
person who makes sound. The model is the kind of DSP blog where every paragraph says something specific.

## Do

- **Say what the thing is in the first paragraph.** Define the technique in one or two sentences, precisely
  enough that a reader could start building it.
- **Be concrete.** Name the parameters, give numbers and units (Hz, ms, cents, dB), describe the steps in order.
- **Describe what it sounds like in ordinary words**, and on what material: "on a solo voice", "on drums",
  "on a full mix".
- **Say where it breaks.** What input it fails on, what artefacts it adds, which settings aren't worth using.
- **Credit sources** by name and, where it matters, year: the person or paper the idea comes from, the tool
  you used, the article that showed you the trick. Link them.
- **Write in first person** when it's your experience or opinion ("I like this on monophonic material").
- **Use full paragraphs.** Lists are for steps, parameters and options, not for rhythm.
- **End when you've said what you have to say.** A practical note or a pointer to the lab is a fine ending.

## Avoid

- Stacked one-sentence paragraphs for drama ("No glossy interfaces. No promises. Just questions.").
- "It's not X, it's Y" reveals, rhetorical questions as headings, and closing aphorisms.
- Hype words that don't carry information: profound, magical, alien, impossible, haunting, mind-bending,
  revolutionary, ghost, sonic universe.
- Emoji as bullet points, and decorative "// LABELS" in capitals for ordinary section breaks.
- Claims about how the brain or "hearing itself" works unless there's a source.
- Describing an experiment you didn't do. If it's an idea, say it's an idea.

## Conventions

- Titles in title case. Dates as YYYY-MM-DD in `meta`.
- Spelling: waveset, wavesets (one word); EDO in capitals (22 EDO, 31 EDO); Hz, kHz, ms, dB, cents.
- Every post with a lab says in one sentence what the lab lets you do.
